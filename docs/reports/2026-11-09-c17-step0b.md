# C17 step 0b: the prototype (2026-11-09)

Spec: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-gpu-rd-pass-design.md (draft). Step 0b asks two things before the
byte-changing step 1:
- is the integer path on the CPU no slower than today's;
- what would the RD kernel cost the GPU a frame, and would the queue stay under 60% busy.

Build 05d89f8 plus `CELV2_TX_DUMP` (rd_pass.cpp) and the throwaway benches in tools/c17. RTX 5090 and Arc Pro B50,
Xeon 658X. Outputs in celv2-bench\c17.

## The dumps

`CELV2_TX_DUMP` writes `tx_cost()`'s inputs (size, TCQ flag, qindex, scale, residual), every 16th call, and each
size's calls and uncached calls at exit. The first 8 frames of S1 are a still picture: every residual is zero, so the
memo answers all but one call a superblock and size. The dumps therefore come from moving parts, 16 frames each:
- S1: mxam600.y4m from frame 200 (s1cut.y4m), 605,881 records;
- Dragon Maid: g_md.y4m from frame 40 (dmcut.y4m), 748,049 records.

`--workers auto --refs 7 --qindex 130 --tune animation --device vulkan`. Every record has TCQ on.

## Verdict

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| The CPU's integer path no slower | **Passes, by far.** Integer transform 0.60-0.71x of today's float time; integer TCQ 0.76x of today's double TCQ (S1). |
| The RD kernel's time a frame (RTX 5090) | Transform and TCQ of every call, no memo: **16.0 ms (S1), 42.1 ms (DM)** with a naive kernel. The Viterbi uses 8 of 64 threads and is 7.3 / 31.4 ms of that. |
| The GPU's projected busy share under 60% with three workers | **Fails with the naive kernel:** about 66-69%. It needs a packed Viterbi (8 blocks a workgroup) or C23's savings first. |
| B50 | 206 / 624 ms a frame: too slow. B50 keeps the CPU path, as the spec's slow-GPU rule says. |

Two measurements change how C17's gain should be judged:
- `--rd-pass 0` is no ceiling: it is slower. S1 runs 6.40 fps against 9.65 with the pass on; DM runs 6.79 against 7.04. Without the pass the coder gets worse decisions and does more work.
- **On this 48-thread machine fps does not follow the RD pass's CPU.** With 16 logical CPUs it does, almost in proportion.

| `CELV2_TX_MEMO` (S1 240 frames) | 48 threads | 16 threads (affinity FFFF) |
|---|---|---|
| on (CPU a frame) | 9.44 fps (2063 ms) | 6.46 fps (1804 ms) |
| off | 9.76 fps (2306 ms) | 5.77 fps (2126 ms) |
| on against off | -3.3% (noise) | **+11.9%** for -15% CPU |

At 48 threads something after the RD pass sets a cap near 9.5-9.7 fps on S1, about 20 of 48 threads busy. The coder
(two tiles at 1080p) and the in-loop filters are the suspects; not measured yet. So:
- **48 threads (this machine):** C17 gives about 0 fps until that cap moves.
- **8 cores / 16 threads (a common desktop):** the RD pass is 63% of S1's CPU a frame. Taking its scoring off the CPU
  could raise S1 from 6.5 fps toward the cap, up to about +45% (estimate, not measured).
- **Step 1 alone** (the CPU rewrite in integers) saves about 170 ms of S1's 2003 ms a frame (estimate from the
  benches: 276 ms of transforms and 318 ms of quantization under the RD pass, times 0.65 and 0.76). That is about
  -8.5% CPU, worth about +9% fps at 16 threads and on `--device cpu`. It changes the bytes, so the BD-rate gate of
  step 1 applies.

## The CPU benches (tools/c17/c17_bench.cpp)

One thread, uncached calls only (the memo stays on the CPU). Time a call times the encode's uncached calls of that
size, over 16 frames. The rows vary by about 10-30% between runs (the machine was not quiet). The totals are steadier.

### The transforms, ms a frame

| | S1 | DM |
|---|---|---|
| float: today's `fwd_dct()` (Lee's butterflies) | 156.6-159.3 | 234.8-259.1 |
| avm: AVM's own AVX2 DCT2 (`fwd_transform_1d_avx2`, int32 even-odd butterflies) | 98.7-112.4 (0.63-0.71x) | 154.8-162.2 (0.60-0.69x) |
| madd16: AVM's matrices as `_mm256_madd_epi16` products, both passes in 16 bits | 108.9-129.3 (0.70-0.81x) | 167.9-192.1 (0.71-0.74x) |

- madd16 matches its scalar reference exactly: 0 of 13.6 M (S1) and 17.6 M (DM) coefficients differ. Its first pass
  is shifted by log2(h) - 1 so its outputs fit 16 bits.
- The integer coefficients differ from `fwd_dct()`'s by 1.2-2.4% of their magnitude (mean |difference| over mean
  |coefficient|). The end of block after quantization is within 0.1-2.5 of float's on average, depending on size.
- The review expected the integer transform to cost 2-2.5x the float one, because the second pass cannot use 16-bit
  butterflies. That is refuted: even the full-matrix madd16 is faster. AVM's int32 butterflies are faster still and
  have no 16-bit limit. **Step 1 should take AVM's own butterflies (int32), with AVM's own shifts**, which also makes
  the GPU's version plain int32 arithmetic.

### The quantizer, ms a frame (S1)

| | double: today's `quantize()` | integer prototype |
|---|---|---|
| total | 181.5-215.8 | 137.3 (0.76x) |

The integer prototype (`int_tcq()`) does the following:
- tcq_levels()'s vectors, blends and tie rules on int64;
- magnitudes in 1/256 steps by a multiply, `(|c| m_q) >> 16` with `m_q = 2^(24+qshift) / q`, in place of the
  divisions;
- squared errors in 1/65536 squared steps;
- bits in 1/512 bit;
- a clamped infinity of 2^60.

Its ends of block equal float's on the same coefficients. DM's quantizer is much heavier: 1167 ms a frame on one
thread for double, mostly 32x32 with a mean end of block of 315. The integer run there was not timed in this pass.

The TCQ Viterbi plan (docs/design/2026-11-09-tcq-viterbi-plan.md) speeds up today's double code exactly. If step 1
goes ahead, its ideas belong in the integer version instead (the U/V state layout, the candidates' pre-pass), so the
work is done once.

## The GPU prototype (tools/c17/c17_tx.slang, c17_gpu.cpp)

One workgroup of 64 threads a block, one size a dispatch:
- the integer DCT2 as full products in groupshared memory;
- the magnitudes and the end of block as `int_tcq()`;
- an eight-state Viterbi on lanes 0-7 (a lane a state, the predecessors' int64 costs read across the subgroup);
- the backtrack and the dropped coefficients' squares.

Its mean end of block equals the CPU's on every size (S1 32x32: 91.8 against 91.8, DM: 315.1 against 315.1), so it
does the same amount of work. Every call is counted (the GPU has no memo). Best of 3 rounds of 4 dispatches,
wall-clock.

| ms a frame | S1 | DM |
|---|---|---|
| RTX 5090, transform + TCQ | 16.0 | 42.1 |
| RTX 5090, transform only | 8.7 | 10.7 |
| Arc Pro B50, transform + TCQ | 206.4 | 623.8 |
| Arc Pro B50, transform only | 102.8 | 129.5 |

Where the time goes:
- 32x32 is the largest part: 6.3 ms of S1's 16.0 and 16.6 ms of DM's 42.1.
- The Viterbi runs one block at a time on 8 lanes, while 56 threads wait.
- Eight blocks a workgroup (a lane a state of each) would use all 64 threads. That should take the Viterbi from 31 ms
  toward 5-8 ms on DM (estimate).
- The prototype leaves out the predictions and residuals that the real kernel would also compute.

## The GPU's busy share today

nvidia-smi's utilization, sampled every 100 ms, the middle 80% of the run, RTX 5090:

| | fps | busy | GPU ms a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 240, 48 threads | 9.66 | 48.8% | 50.5 |
| S1 240, 16 threads | 6.53 | 30.9% | 47.3 |
| DM 120, 48 threads | 6.95 | 32.5% | 46.8 |

`CELV2_VK_TIMES` gives about the same: 47.2 ms a frame (S1) and 53.3 ms (DM). The time stamps' barriers inflate
these numbers less than feared.

The engine's kernels a frame (S1): cost inter 21.4 ms, finalize 14.5, me wide 7.2, cost intra 3.4.

Projected busy share with the RD kernel, at the fps the encoder would then reach:
- naive kernel, plus about 5 ms for predictions:
  - S1: (50.5 + 21) x 9.5 fps = 68%;
  - DM: (47 + 47) x 7 = 66%.
- packed Viterbi and a lighter transform (estimate, RD 13-20 ms):
  - S1: about 60%;
  - DM: about 47%.
- C23's plan for cost inter and finalize (36 ms of S1's 50) is the other lever.

## What next (for the user)

1. **Step 1, the CPU rewrite in integers.** It is a CPU gain by itself (about -8.5% CPU a frame on S1, estimate),
   it is the base for the GPU and for Q1, and the integer forms are measured faster. It changes the bytes once:
   BD-rate gate as in the spec.
2. **Find the 48-thread cap** (about 9.6 fps on S1) before more CPU work: on this machine no CPU saving in the RD pass
   shows in fps until it moves. A new speed item.
3. **Step 2, the GPU port,** only after the Viterbi is packed in the prototype and the projected busy share is under
   60% (or C23 lands first). Its fps gain is on CPUs of 8-16 threads, not on this one.

## Reproduce

Run from celv2-bench\c17:

```bash
python -I tools/c17/y4mcut.py sources/mxam600.y4m s1cut.y4m 200 16
CELV2_TX_DUMP=$PWD/S1.dump celv2enc -i s1cut.y4m -o S1.ivf --workers auto --refs 7 --qindex 130 --tune animation --device vulkan
c17_bench S1.dump 16 3
c17_gpu S1.dump 16 0 5
```

For c17_gpu: device 1 is the B50, and a fifth argument of 1 skips the Viterbi.

The bytes are unchanged by `CELV2_TX_DUMP`, set or not. s1cut and dmcut give the same md5 as 05d89f8 on Vulkan with
`--workers auto` and on `--device cpu --workers 1`.
